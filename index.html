Part 1: Proposal

What we plan to research and make.

Summary

Our team will research how communication styles differ across cultures. We will focus on three areas: direct vs. indirect communication, high-context vs. low-context cultures, and verbal vs. nonverbal differences. Our final deliverable is a short educational guide that teaches readers how to handle these differences with respect. We are aiming for an A-level project.

Why we picked this topic

Most of us already work or study with people from different backgrounds. A message that sounds clear to one person can sound rude or vague to another. We want to understand why that happens and what to do about it.

The topic also lets us practice the 11 communication strategies in real situations. Clarifying, Questioning, and Repairing fit most directly. We will also practice Investigating, because we have to find good sources and judge them. Working as a team on a project about communication means we will be using these skills as we go.

Final deliverable

We will make a 6 to 8 page illustrated guide called Say It So They Get It: A Practical Guide to Cross Cultural Communication. We chose a guide because it can do two jobs. It explains what the research says, and it shows readers what to do about it.

The guide will hold our summaries, comparison charts, original examples, and strategy tips in one place. We’ll create it in Google Docs first, then convert it into HTML.

Tentative outline

  1. Introduction. Why culture changes the way we communicate.
  2. Direct vs. indirect communication. What each looks like, with a comparison chart.
  3. High-context vs. low-context cultures. Key ideas and where they show up.
  4. Verbal vs. nonverbal differences. Tone, silence, eye contact, gestures, and personal space.
  5. Where things go wrong. A comparison of the challenges each difference creates.
  6. Our original examples. One short scenario for each of the three areas.
  7. The 11 strategies in action. How strategies like Clarifying, Questioning, and Repairing help us handle these differences.
  8. Quick tips and references. A one-page takeaway for readers, then our sources.

Part 3: Team ground rules

What we expect from each other. We will use these to check in on ourselves in the Progress Update and Team Review.

  1. Reply to team messages within 24 hours.Silence slows everyone down. Quick replies keep us in the loop.
  2. Tell the team early if you will miss a meeting or deadline.Early notice lets us move work around instead of scrambling.
  3. Finish your tasks by the agreed date.Our grade depends on everyone's part, so we treat deadlines as promises.
  4. Be respectful when we disagree.We talk about ideas, not people, and we listen before we respond. This matters even more on a project about communication.
  5. Everyone contributes to every assignment.We split work fairly based on skills and schedules so no one carries the team.

If someone breaks a rule: we check in privately first. If it keeps happening, we bring it to the instructor.
